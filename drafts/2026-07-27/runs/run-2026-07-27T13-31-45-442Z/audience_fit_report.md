## Story comparison

### OpenAI Presence turns agents into a managed workplace product

- Audience: Small business owners, team leads, operations managers, and creators who want AI to save time without losing control.
- Interest signal: OpenAI says Presence is available to eligible enterprise customers, and deployments are led by engineers and systems integrators.
- Stop reason: It answers the concrete question of how AI moves from chat to a governed workflow.
- Payoff: See what a managed AI agent looks like and what to define before automating a real task.
- Proof: OpenAI says Presence is available today to eligible enterprise customers through a limited general availability program, with approvals, guardrails, and human escalation built into deployment.
- Overall score: 8/10
- Reason: Strongest mix of concrete workplace consequence, clear verified detail, and easy-to-explain shift from chat tools to managed systems.

### Google’s ATLAS report shows where AI is actually being used at work

- Audience: Workers, creators, analysts, and managers who want a data-backed view of current AI use.
- Interest signal: Google says ATLAS v1.0 is built from 15 million aggregated and de-identified interactions across Gemini products.
- Stop reason: It gives a broad snapshot of workplace AI use across countries, languages, occupations, and tasks.
- Payoff: See which tasks dominate current AI use and where first-use cases cluster.
- Proof: Google says the dataset spans more than 150 countries, 140 languages, 800 occupations, and 4,000 tasks.
- Overall score: 8/10
- Reason: Useful and credible, but less immediately punchy than the managed-workflow or productivity-gain stories.

### Gallup’s latest workplace data says adoption is widening, but use is still uneven

- Audience: Managers, small business owners, and employees choosing which AI task to test first.
- Interest signal: Gallup says more than half of U.S. workers now use AI in their role, with writing, research, and problem-solving leading usage.
- Stop reason: It ties adoption to specific job functions and measurable payoff.
- Payoff: Identify a task that is frequent, boring, and easy to review, then pilot AI there first.
- Proof: Gallup says the strongest productivity gains are linked to more task-specific uses such as coding, automation, analytics, and slide creation.
- Overall score: 9/10
- Reason: Most directly actionable for a broad work audience because it links adoption to a simple pilot choice and measurable outcomes.

### The UK is starting to treat AI training as a workplace capability issue

- Audience: Employers, HR leads, operations managers, and small business owners who need a simple AI training plan.
- Interest signal: The UK government says AI is becoming embedded in everyday working life and proposes PRIMES for workforce training.
- Stop reason: It turns AI from a software purchase into a training and role-design problem.
- Payoff: Set a simple internal policy, choose approved tools, and run a short training session around one workflow.
- Proof: The UK government says the SKAI programme proposes PRIMES as a framework for inclusive, safe, and sustainable AI workforce training.
- Overall score: 8/10
- Reason: Practical and credible, with strong employer relevance, though it is narrower and less visually immediate than the top two stories.

## Overall reasoning

The evidence points to one clear theme: AI at work is moving from experimentation to managed, reviewable workflows. For short-form attention, the strongest hook is the shift from chat to governed agents; for broader platforms, the best response is practical and task-specific. The selections favor stories that let a stranger answer with a real workflow, a real team decision, or a real training need without needing extra context.
