What has changed is the baseline expectation of privacy. Now, the burden has shifted entirely to the creator. If you do not manually navigate to your settings and toggle this off, your content is effectively being harvested for model development. If you are a professional streamer, your content is your business. Allowing a platform to ingest that data without your explicit, informed consent can have long-term implications for how your work is replicated or synthesized by automated systems. Who should care about this? Everyone who streams on Twitch, regardless of their audience size. Whether you are a hobbyist or a full-time professional, your data is being treated as a resource for model training. This does not affect your channel safety tools, your moderation settings, or your ability to stream. It is purely about data rights. For those of you who want to test the impact of these settings, I suggest a simple experiment. Review your current content archives. If you have years of VODs, consider which ones you want to remain private or restricted. Ask yourself if your content is being used in ways that align with your personal brand values. If you are a small business owner using Twitch for marketing, this is even more critical. The primary risk is the loss of control over your creative identity. We are seeing a trend where platforms prioritize their own AI development needs over the granular preferences of their users. This is why human review is so important. You cannot rely on the platform to protect your interests; you must be the one to set the boundaries. As for our verdict on this, our recommendation is to opt out immediately. This is a case where the default setting does not serve the creator. Until there is more transparency about how this data is used and what safeguards are in place to prevent the misuse of your likeness, it is safer to keep your content out of these training pools. Stay vigilant, keep your settings updated, and always prioritize the protection of your creative work. This is the reality of the current digital landscape. It is not about being anti-AI; it is about being pro-creator. ## Plain-English terms

- VOD: Video on Demand, which refers to your past stream archives.
- Generative AI: Systems that create new content like text, images, or audio based on patterns learned from existing data.
- Opt-in/Opt-out: The choice to either join a program or specifically request to be removed from it.
- Data harvesting: The process of collecting large amounts of information from users to train AI models.
- Intellectual Property: Your creative work, including your voice, style, and original content, which you own.
