# How to adopt AI tools that work inside the apps your team already uses

**Short direct answer**

Adopt them as workflow tools, not as novelty features. The best use cases are the ones where AI helps inside a familiar app to draft, search, summarise, label, route or prepare work — while a person still reviews anything that will be sent, published, scheduled, or used for a decision.

## What it means

The big shift is that AI is moving from a separate chat box into the tools people already use every day. That includes search, documents, design, meetings, support systems, notebooks and project boards.

In the approved research, this shows up in several ways:

- **OpenAI** says ChatGPT Work can use local files and desktop apps with permission, and the desktop app brings Chat, Work and Codex into one place.
- **Google** says AI Mode in Search can connect to apps like Instacart, Canva and YouTube Music, so a task can start in search and continue in another app.
- **Canva** says its AI 2.0 preview adds connectors, scheduling, web research, brand intelligence and Sheets AI, so a project can move from idea to published output in one place.
- **Notion** says users can assign external agents such as Claude and Cursor from a shared board, making the handoff visible.
- **Meta** says Meta AI can make plans, connect to calendar and email apps, create slides and handle tasks on a user’s behalf.

The practical meaning is simple: AI is no longer only about generating text. It is being used to move work through a workflow.

That is useful, but it also creates new control points. Once a tool can send, schedule, publish, or edit live work, review and permissions matter much more than before.

## How it works in practice

A good rollout starts by mapping one repeatable workflow and asking where AI fits best.

A useful pattern is:

1. **Collect the source material**
   - meeting notes
   - briefs
   - files
   - source links
   - customer messages

2. **Let AI prepare a first pass**
   - draft a summary
   - extract action items
   - reorganise a doc
   - suggest a reply
   - create a slide or post draft

3. **Keep a human review step**
   - check the sources
   - check the claim
   - check the tone
   - check the permissions
   - check the destination before send or publish

4. **Label or log what AI touched**
   - especially for ads, images, video, external communications and regulated content

This is consistent with the direction of the source material. Google says its ad transparency tools add a “How this ad was made” panel and label ads made or edited with generative AI. The European Commission’s guidance for AI Act transparency duties says disclosure and machine-readable marking become operational requirements, not just policy language. Meta says its generated images carry a hidden Content Seal watermark, and Google says generated clips in Vids include SynthID watermarks.

In other words, disclosure is becoming part of the workflow, not a cleanup step.

## Why organisations are adopting it

Organisations are not adopting embedded AI because it is trendy. They are doing it because it can reduce friction in ordinary work.

The main reasons in the research are:

- **Less app switching.** Search can hand a task into another app; documents, design and notes can stay connected.
- **Faster first drafts.** AI can produce a usable starting point for summaries, emails, slides, reports or social assets.
- **More consistent process handling.** Tools like Notion boards, Jira workflows and Google Meet admin settings make AI part of an explicit system.
- **Better fit with existing permissions.** Zoom says AI On-Prem can run on customer infrastructure or private cloud; OpenAI says ChatGPT Work can use local files and desktop apps with permission.
- **More transparency pressure.** Google’s ad labels and the EU transparency guidance show that disclosure is becoming a normal expectation.

There is also a human reason. Thomson Reuters found that one in three professionals are using AI that was not officially approved. That does not prove AI works better; it proves people are already using it informally. Organisations adopt embedded AI partly to bring that usage into a governed workflow.

## What changes for people and workflows

The biggest change is that AI moves from “help me draft” to “help me move work forward.”

That changes the human role in four ways:

### 1. Review becomes more important

When a tool can create, send, schedule, or publish, the cost of a mistake rises. A human needs to check source, claim, tone and destination before work leaves the system.

### 2. Permissions matter more

AI features often need access to files, calendar, email, boards or connected apps. That access should be limited to what the workflow needs. If a feature can act on behalf of a user, you should know what it can do without a fresh approval step.

### 3. Documentation becomes part of the task

If AI helped produce an ad, image, video, or customer-facing document, keep a record of what changed and what label or watermark applies. Google’s ad transparency panel and SynthID examples show why this matters.

### 4. Managers need to define the handoff

The question is no longer only “can the model do it?” It is “where does AI stop and human control begin?”

That is the cleanest operating rule in the research: automate the draft, not the approval.

## Limits, risks and what remains uncertain

There are real gains here, but the evidence is still uneven.

### What is established

- AI use is already broad. The Office for National Statistics said self-reported AI use in UK businesses with 10 or more employees rose to around 35% by June 2026, up from around 12% in late 2023.
- People are using AI even when it is not officially approved. Thomson Reuters found one-third of professionals reported that.
- Vendors are adding transparency, watermarks and guardrails.

### What is still uncertain

- **Rollout scope is often not fully disclosed.** Some features are limited to select markets, certain plans, or phased releases. For example, Meta said rollout of its task-based AI started in select markets on 24 July and will expand later. Google and OpenAI also have plan and region differences in their launches.
- **Outcome data is thin.** Vendors project time savings and better workflow control, but the approved research does not give broad independent measurement of productivity gains or error reduction across many teams.
- **Autonomy can outpace review.** A tool that can act on a user’s behalf can also make it easier to send something too early.
- **Compliance rules are still moving.** The EU AI Omnibus changed some timelines, and the Commission’s transparency guidance starts applying on 2 August 2026. Teams should not treat this as a final settled state.

### Practical risk categories

- wrong draft sent externally
- unapproved content disclosure failure
- accidental use of sensitive files
- unclear ownership of AI-generated outputs
- hidden vendor lock-in
- overreliance on an assistant that has not been tested on your exact task

## Practical questions to ask before using it

Before you switch on an embedded AI feature, ask:

1. **What exact task does this help with?**
   - drafting, summarising, scheduling, routing, searching, or publishing?

2. **What data does it need?**
   - files, email, calendar, chats, board content, customer data?

3. **Can it act without a fresh approval?**
   - can it send, post, schedule, assign, or change records on its own?

4. **Where does the review step live?**
   - in the doc, board, inbox, ad manager, or meeting summary?

5. **How will we label or log AI use?**
   - especially for ads, images, video, client work and public content

6. **Can we export the output and the audit trail?**
   - if you cannot explain how the output was made, it is harder to trust

7. **What is the fallback if the tool is wrong or unavailable?**
   - can the team keep working without it?

8. **Is the rollout complete?**
   - or is the feature still a preview, limited release, or plan-specific option?

## Current examples

These are useful current examples of the pattern, not universal standards:

- **ChatGPT Work**: OpenAI says it can use local files and desktop apps with permission, and the desktop app combines Chat, Work and Codex.
- **Google AI Mode and Search-connected apps**: Google says Search can connect to apps like Instacart, Canva and YouTube Music.
- **Google Vids**: Google says eligible users can generate and edit clips, create personal avatars, and get SynthID watermarks on generated clips.
- **Canva AI 2.0**: Canva says its research preview adds connectors, scheduling, web research, brand intelligence, Sheets AI and Canva Code 2.0.
- **Notion 3.6**: Notion says users can assign external agents from a shared board, with speaker labels in meeting notes.
- **Meta AI**: Meta says it can make plans, use calendar and email context, create slides and handle tasks on a user’s behalf.
- **Zoom AI On-Prem**: Zoom says AI workloads can run on customer infrastructure or private cloud.
- **Salesforce Agentforce Help Agent**: Salesforce says it is packaged with pay-per-resolution pricing, which shows how AI is being sold as an operational workflow.
- **Xero JAX**: Xero says its AI platform is adding document capture and workflow automation for small businesses and accountants.

These examples point in the same direction: AI is being placed closer to the point where work gets approved and handed off.
