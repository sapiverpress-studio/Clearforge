Today's briefing covers significant regulatory shifts in New Zealand, major AI capital movements, and the widening investment gap between US and European corporate infrastructure.

lead · Tracy Withers/Bloomberg

New Zealand Proposes Social Media Ban for Under-16s

What happened: The New Zealand government has introduced the Online Safety (Minimum Age and Child Safety Risk Assessment) Bill, which would mandate age verification on high-risk platforms and impose penalties of up to 10% of global revenue for non-compliance.

Why it matters: The bill represents a significant escalation in global regulatory efforts to restrict youth access to social media, with substantial financial implications for tech firms operating in the region.

Our read: Our read is that the bill's success remains uncertain, as it currently lacks a guaranteed majority in the coalition government, making its passage dependent on opposition support.

Nvidia in Talks for $30B+ Perplexity Investment

What happened: Nvidia is reportedly in negotiations to invest in an equity round for AI search startup Perplexity, valuing the company at over $30 billion.

Why it matters: This potential investment signals Nvidia's continued interest in deepening its footprint within the AI search and enterprise agent ecosystem.

Our read: The move suggests Nvidia may be shifting from a strategy of potential talent and licensing deals toward direct equity stakes in high-growth AI search platforms.

technology · Financial Times

US AI Infrastructure Spending Outpaces Europe 3x

What happened: Oxford Economics projects US corporate spending on equipment and facilities will rise 40% between 2021 and 2027, a growth rate more than three times higher than that of Europe, largely driven by AI investment.

Why it matters: The disparity highlights a widening gap in capital allocation toward AI-ready infrastructure, which could influence long-term regional competitiveness in technology deployment.

Our read: This trend suggests that US firms may be moving more aggressively to integrate AI at the physical infrastructure level compared to their European counterparts.

ai · Ina Fried/Axios

Meta Recruits OpenAI Researcher Luke Metz

What happened: Luke Metz, a key contributor to the initial launch of ChatGPT, has departed OpenAI to join Meta's Superintelligence Labs, reporting to Alexandr Wang.

Why it matters: The movement of high-profile researchers between frontier AI organizations remains a critical indicator of talent concentration and competitive positioning in the race for advanced AI development.

Our read: This hire may indicate Meta's continued focus on strengthening its internal research capabilities for superintelligence projects.

Monitor the legislative progress of the New Zealand bill as a potential bellwether for future age-verification requirements in other jurisdictions.
