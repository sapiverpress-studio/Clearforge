Today's briefing covers a significant legal victory for Anthropic, new U.S. export control developments, and major capital shifts in AI infrastructure and enterprise software.

lead · nytimes.com

Judge rules U.S. blacklisting of Anthropic illegal

What happened: U.S. District Judge Rita Lin ruled that the Trump administration acted illegally by designating Anthropic a national security supply chain risk and barring federal agencies from working with the company.

Why it matters: The ruling challenges the government's authority to use national security designations as a tool for retaliation against AI firms, potentially setting a precedent for future regulatory interactions with the sector.

Our read: The decision highlights the ongoing friction between federal oversight and private AI development, suggesting that administrative actions against tech firms face significant judicial scrutiny regarding constitutional protections.

technology · The Information

U.S. drafting rules to restrict remote AI chip access

What happened: The U.S. Commerce Department is reportedly drafting export control rules to prevent Chinese companies from accessing advanced U.S. AI chips via cloud data centers in third-party countries.

Why it matters: This move signals an attempt to close a critical loophole in existing chip export controls, which could impact the operational models of global cloud providers and their international data center strategies.

Our read: The proposed regulation suggests a shift toward governing compute access rather than just physical hardware shipments, reflecting the difficulty of enforcing technology restrictions in a globalised cloud environment.

a16z launches $1.1B 'Machine Age' fund

What happened: Andreessen Horowitz has raised a $1.1 billion fund specifically targeted at AI hardware, robotics, and physical infrastructure.

Why it matters: The fund represents a significant capital commitment toward the 'physical' side of the AI stack, moving beyond software-only investment models.

Our read: This allocation indicates a growing venture capital focus on the industrial and hardware requirements necessary to support long-term AI scaling.

ai · Reuters

Workday reports strong AI agent adoption

What happened: Workday reported Q2 revenue of $2.65 billion, with AI agent products accounting for over 25% of all new Annual Contract Value (ACV).

Why it matters: These figures provide tangible evidence of enterprise-level monetization of AI agents, moving beyond experimental use cases into core business software revenue.

Our read: The data suggests that enterprise customers are increasingly willing to pay for AI-driven automation, providing a potential benchmark for software companies looking to integrate AI agents into existing workflows.
