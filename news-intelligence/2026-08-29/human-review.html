Today's briefing covers the escalating corporate tensions between OpenAI and SpaceX, a significant legal victory for Anthropic, and the shifting landscape of global semiconductor manufacturing.

lead · Reuters

OpenAI to terminate model access for SpaceX-owned Cursor

What happened: OpenAI has notified SpaceX that it will cut off AI model access for the Cursor coding assistant by November 12, 2026, citing a change-of-control clause and contractual trust concerns.

Why it matters: This move highlights the fragility of AI infrastructure dependencies when corporate ownership changes, potentially disrupting workflows for developers relying on Cursor's multi-model platform.

Our read: The decision suggests that OpenAI is actively enforcing strict contractual boundaries regarding its technology's availability to entities associated with Elon Musk.

ai · nytimes.com

Court rules government blacklisting of Anthropic unlawful

What happened: A U.S. District Judge ruled that the Department of Defense's designation of Anthropic as a national security risk was an unlawful retaliatory act that violated First Amendment rights.

Why it matters: The ruling limits the government's ability to use supply chain blacklisting as a tool to pressure AI companies into accepting specific contractual terms regarding model deployment.

Our read: This sets a significant legal precedent for how AI labs can challenge government oversight when they refuse to comply with state-mandated restrictions on their technology.

technology · Bloomberg

SK Hynix breaks ground on $4B Indiana memory facility

What happened: SK Hynix has begun construction on an advanced memory packaging plant in Indiana, supported by $458 million in U.S. CHIPS Act funding, with volume production expected in late 2029.

Why it matters: The facility represents a strategic effort to localise the back-end packaging of High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) within the U.S., addressing critical supply chain bottlenecks for AI hardware.

Our read: The investment indicates a long-term commitment to integrating U.S.-based operations into the global HBM supply chain, despite the multi-year timeline for full production.

business · Summer Zhen/Reuters

Xiaomi to debut domestic LPDDR6 memory in new flagship

What happened: Xiaomi's upcoming 18 Fold smartphone will feature CXMT's LPDDR6 DRAM chips and an in-house 3nm Xring O3 SoC, marking a milestone for Chinese-developed memory technology.

Why it matters: The deployment of domestic LPDDR6 memory in a flagship device signals a potential shift in the competitive landscape for mobile memory, challenging established suppliers like Samsung and Micron.

Our read: This development suggests that Chinese manufacturers are successfully accelerating the commercialisation of advanced memory components to reduce reliance on international vendors.
