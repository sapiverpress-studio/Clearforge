Today's briefing covers the intersection of physical infrastructure and AI, regulatory shifts for open-source software, and the evolving landscape of AI-integrated business models.

lead · Raffaele Huang/Wall Street Journal

Nvidia’s physical AI segment reaches $10B annual revenue

What happened: Nvidia’s physical AI business, which includes robotics and autonomous systems, now generates $10B in annual revenue. Chinese robotics developers remain heavily reliant on Nvidia’s hardware and software stack despite broader export restrictions.

Why it matters: The reliance of international robotics firms on a single US-based technology stack highlights the critical role of specialized silicon in the physical automation sector.

Our read: The growth of this segment suggests that Nvidia's influence extends well beyond data center LLM training, positioning the company as a foundational layer for global industrial robotics.

technology · Ann Davis Vaughan/The Information

SpaceX builds in-house foundry to accelerate data center power supply

What happened: SpaceX is establishing an in-house foundry to produce nickel-superalloy turbine blades and vanes. The initiative aims to bypass global supply chain backlogs to bring natural gas turbines for AI data centers online up to 18 months faster.

Why it matters: Vertical integration of critical hardware components could provide a significant speed advantage for entities managing massive AI infrastructure, such as xAI.

Our read: This move indicates that power infrastructure, rather than just compute availability, is becoming a primary bottleneck for large-scale AI deployment.

technology · Luke James/Tom's Hardware

California passes bill exempting open-source OS from age-verification mandates

What happened: The California Legislature unanimously passed AB 1856, which explicitly exempts open-source operating systems distributed under permissive or copyleft licenses from mandatory age-verification requirements.

Why it matters: The exemption provides legal clarity for open-source projects, preventing them from being burdened by compliance costs that would otherwise be prohibitive for community-led development.

Our read: The unanimous vote suggests a legislative recognition of the distinct operational model of open-source software compared to proprietary commercial platforms.

ai · @claudedevs

Anthropic adjusts Claude Code usage limits

What happened: Anthropic is transitioning to a permanent 25% increase in weekly Claude Code limits starting September 14, 2026. Because this replaces a temporary 50% promotional boost, users will experience a net 17% reduction in capacity.

Why it matters: Frequent adjustments to usage caps in AI-integrated development tools can disrupt workflows for enterprise teams relying on consistent capacity.

Our read: The backlash to the initial framing of this change highlights the sensitivity of professional users to capacity fluctuations in AI-assisted coding environments.

Practical takeaway

When scaling AI operations, monitor both compute availability and the physical infrastructure supply chain; meanwhile, developers should anticipate capacity fluctuations in AI-coding tools and ensure compliance strategies account for evolving regional OS regulations.

What to watch next

Monitor the operational ramp-up of SpaceX's turbine foundry and its impact on the deployment timelines of large-scale AI data centers.
