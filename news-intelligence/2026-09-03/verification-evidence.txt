### Strongest Candidates: Evidence Notes

#### Candidate [2]: Google Defeats DOJ Bid for Ad Tech Breakup
* **What is confirmed:** On September 2, 2026, US District Judge Leonie Brinkema in Alexandria, Virginia, issued an order rejecting the Department of Justice's attempt to force Google to divest its online ad exchange (AdX). Following an earlier liability ruling that Google held illegal monopolies in ad tech markets, the court opted for behavioral remedies (requiring interoperability with competing ad tools) rather than structural breakup.
* **Important caveats:** The court’s detailed memorandum opinion remains sealed for 14 days. The DOJ and Google have 30 days to confer and submit a joint proposed final judgment. The DOJ noted it is evaluating next steps (including potential appeals).
* **Publication status:** **Suitable for publication today.** This is the primary regulatory story of the day.

#### Candidates [10] & [11]: G20 Endorses US-Led "Carolina Principles" for Light-Touch AI Regulation
* **What is confirmed:** At the conclusion of the two-day G20 Innovation Ministerial in Chapel Hill, North Carolina (September 1–2, 2026), G20 member nations adopted a joint statement endorsing the "Carolina Principles for Emerging Technologies". Proposed by US OSTP Director Michael Kratsios and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, the non-binding framework urges governments to reserve new regulations for genuinely novel issues, avoid establishing dedicated AI oversight agencies, and focus on foundational research and commercial growth. Top tech executives—including Elon Musk, Mark Zuckerberg, Sam Altman, Jensen Huang, and Demis Hassabis—attended and lobbied against heavy-handed rules.
* **Important caveats:** The statement is non-binding and feeds into the G20 leaders' summit in December. EU representatives attending the meeting noted that the European Commission is continuing to enforce the EU AI Act and issuing formal information requests to AI labs, underscoring persistent transatlantic divergence.
* **Publication status:** **Suitable for publication today.** High-value global policy story.

#### Candidate [9]: Investigation Reveals 215k Manufactured Pages Gaming Perplexity AI Search
* **What is confirmed:** Trellner Research published an empirical report (TR-2026-009) on September 2, 2026, examining citation sources used by Perplexity’s Sonar and Sonar Pro models across 380 software categories. The study found that 59.8% of citations pointed to domains outside the Tranco top 100,000. Crucially, three interconnected automated template sites accounted for 215,128 machine-generated "best software" pages designed specifically to capture grounding queries in AI retrieval engines.
* **Important caveats:** The study specifically measured Perplexity's API models and explicitly noted that results cannot be generalized to other retrieval-augmented engines without separate auditing. A low domain rank does not automatically prove factual inaccuracy, though mass-generating pages for AI grounding poses clear search quality concerns.
* **Publication status:** **Suitable for publication today.** Excellent original research coverage.

#### Candidate [1]: US Section 232 Drone Tariffs Take Effect
* **What is confirmed:** On September 3, 2026, Section 232 tariffs imposed by the US administration officially took effect. Importers face tariffs up to 100% on foreign drones exceeding 25 kg (55 lbs), models equipped with thermal imaging, and docking stations. Smaller non-sensitive commercial drones face a 25% tariff. Certified allied manufacturers receive lower capped rates (EU/Japan 15%, UK 10%), leaving Chinese manufacturers (e.g. DJI, Autel) facing full duties.
* **Important caveats:** President Trump signed the original proclamation on August 13, 2026; September 3 is the effective implementation date. The NYT feature focuses on the implementation impact and accompanying FCC considerations regarding thermal and agricultural spraying drone technologies [1].
* **Publication status:** **Suitable for publication today.**
